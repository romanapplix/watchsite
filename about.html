Preserving the Art of Time

Collector, Historian & Restorer

Welcome to my vault. My fascination with mechanical timekeeping began over two decades ago when I first held an unserviced pocket watch from the late 19th century. Hearing the syncopated, slow heartbeat of a mechanical escapement ticking after decades of neglect sparked a lifelong obsession with horological preservation.

As a private enthusiast and restorer, my philosophy focuses deeply on provenance, absolute historical accuracy, and the preservation of original patination. I believe that a vintage watch is not merely an instrument of utility; it is a canvas of micro-engineering that captures the precise cultural aesthetic and metallurgical limitations of the era in which it was forged.

"Modern timepieces tell you the time, but vintage timepieces tell you a story. Every tool mark left by a master watchmaker, every faded tritium plot, and every scratch on a caseback serves as a physical archive of human connection."

Beyond curation, my workshop focuses on the collection and study of historic watchmaker tools. Sourcing and utilizing original 1950s staking sets, Bergeon lathed fixtures, and vintage mainspring winders allows me to interface with these mechanical works of art using the exact physical methods of their creators.
